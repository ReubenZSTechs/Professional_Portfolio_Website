## The problem

Assessing vehicle damage, for insurance claims, rentals or used-car inspections, usually means a person studying photos and writing a description by hand. That is slow and inconsistent between assessors. This project answers four questions from one photo: is the car damaged, where, how badly, and how would you describe it?

## How it works

<Pipeline steps={["Car photo", "ResNet-50: damaged?", "YOLOv8: location", "YOLOv8: severity", "ViT + GPT-2 caption", "Streamlit result"]} highlight={1} />

1. A custom **ResNet-50 classifier** decides whether the car is damaged.
2. If it is, two fine-tuned **YOLOv8** models run: one localises the damage (front, back, rear-left, rear-right) and one rates its severity (low, medium, high), each with bounding boxes.
3. A **ViT encoder + GPT-2 decoder** writes a caption describing the car.
4. All four models are combined into one inference pipeline behind a **Streamlit** app, with an optional CUDA toggle.

<Figure src="/images/projects/car-damage-detection-captioning-demo.webp" alt="Streamlit app showing a damaged car with a front-damage bounding box and the results: damaged, probability 1.00, severity high, and a generated caption" caption="The Streamlit demo: detection boxes, severity and the generated caption for one photo." width={740} height={690} />

The dataset was curated from three public sources (1,000+ images) and relabelled in Roboflow, with scripts that split it into train/validation/test and convert labels to YOLO format.

## Key decisions

<Decisions>
  <Decision title="Gate the expensive models">
    The cheap classifier runs first. The two detectors only run when there is damage, which saves compute on undamaged cars and avoids false-positive boxes.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="Separate detectors for location and severity">
    Location and severity are different labelling problems, so each got its own YOLOv8 model instead of one detector with combined classes.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="Captioning as its own stage">
    An encoder-decoder captioner (ViT + GPT-2) turns the visual result into text a non-expert can read, which is the output an assessor actually needs.
  </Decision>
</Decisions>

## Results

<Metrics>
  <Metric value="91.3%" label="Damaged vs undamaged accuracy (ResNet-50)" />
  <Metric value="97.1%" label="ROC-AUC, damaged vs undamaged" />
  <Metric value="+9% / +3%" label="Accuracy / precision over the baseline" />
</Metrics>

PR-AUC for the classifier is 97.49%. The detectors are the weak point: mAP50-95 ranges from 0.04 to 0.15 for location and 0.03 to 0.20 for severity, and the captioner reached a test loss of 1.075. That is why the next steps focus on detection.

## What I'd do next

- Replace YOLOv8 with a stronger detector and collect more labelled damage data for localisation and severity.
- Fine-tune a BLIP captioning model for richer, damage-aware captions.
- Speed up model loading and package the pipeline in Docker for mobile inference.
